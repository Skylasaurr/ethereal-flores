Floribunda Roses

(Rosa x Hybrida)

The name floribunda roughly means “abundant flowering.” This modern type of rose bears clusters of blooms instead of a single flower on each stem. It was developed by crossing hybrid tea roses with polyanthas, bringing together elegant, large flowers, resilience, and generous blooming.

China Roses

(Rosa chinensis)

China roses are among the key ancestors of modern garden roses. Native to China, they reached Europe in the late 1700s and brought a valuable trait: the ability to bloom repeatedly through the season, which most European roses lacked. Their slender, loosely petaled flowers often deepen in color as they age.

Shrub Roses

(Rosa spp.)

Shrub roses are vigorous, bushy plants that grow into rounded or arching mounds. The group is broad, covering many modern and English-style hybrids bred for hardiness, disease resistance, and repeat blooming, which makes them popular for borders, hedges, and relaxed informal gardens.

Hybrid Tea Roses

(Rosa x hybrida)

The classic florist’s rose, the hybrid tea bears a single large, high-centered bloom on a long stem. The type came from crossing hybrid perpetual roses with tea roses, and ‘La France’, introduced in 1867, is often considered the first hybrid tea and the start of the modern rose era.

Ground Cover Roses

(Rosa spp.)

Ground cover roses grow low and spread wide, forming dense mats of foliage and flowers instead of tall upright bushes. They are valued for covering slopes and bare patches, spilling over walls, and needing far less care than many other rose types.

Grandiflora Roses

(Rosa x grandiflora)

Grandifloras came from crossing hybrid teas with floribundas, combining the elegant flower form of the first with the generous clusters of the second. The class was introduced in the 1950s with the rose ‘Queen Elizabeth’, and its plants tend to be tall, strong, and free-flowering.

Alba Roses

(Rosa x alba)

Alba roses are among the oldest garden roses, grown in Europe for many centuries and thought to descend from a cross involving the dog rose. They are known for gray-green foliage, soft white to pale pink fragrant blooms, and a hardy nature that tolerates some shade.

Gallica Roses

(Rosa gallica)

Gallica roses descend from Rosa gallica, one of the oldest cultivated roses in Europe. They produce richly fragrant flowers in deep crimson, magenta, and purple shades, bloom once in early summer, and were historically prized for perfume and medicine as well as beauty.

African Daisies

(Arctotis spp.)

Native to southern Africa, African daisies are sun-loving plants with silvery foliage and vivid flowers in orange, pink, yellow, and white. Their blooms open in bright sunshine and tend to close in the evening or on cloudy days.

Aster Daisies

(Aster spp.)

The name aster comes from the Greek word for “star,” a nod to the starry shape of the flowers. These daisy-like blooms come in purple, pink, blue, and white, and appear in late summer and autumn when many other plants are fading, which makes them an important late source of nectar for pollinators.

Gaillardia Daisies

(Gaillardia x grandiflora)

Commonly called blanket flowers, gaillardias are native to the Americas and are known for fiery red petals tipped in yellow, a pattern said to resemble woven blankets. They thrive in heat and poor, dry soil and bloom for a long stretch through summer.

Blue Daisies

(Felicia amelloides)

Also known as the blue marguerite, this South African native bears sky-blue petals around a bright yellow center. It flowers over a long season in warm climates, and in cooler regions it is usually grown as an annual in pots and borders.

Cape Daisies

(Osteospermum ecklonis)

Cape daisies come from southern Africa and are loved for their bold flowers in white, purple, pink, and yellow, often with a contrasting darker center. Like many of their relatives, the blooms close at night and in shade, and the plants do best in full sun.

Coneflower Daisies

(Echinacea purpurea)

Native to eastern and central North America, the coneflower takes its genus name from the Greek word for hedgehog, a reference to its spiky central cone. It has a long history of traditional medicinal use, draws butterflies and bees in summer, and leaves seed heads that feed birds in autumn.

Dahlberg Daisies

(Thymophylla tenuiloba)

A small, cheerful native of Texas and Mexico, the Dahlberg daisy forms low mounds of finely cut, aromatic foliage covered in tiny golden-yellow flowers. It handles heat and dry conditions well, making it a favorite for rock gardens, edges, and containers.

English Daisies

(Bellis perennis)

The familiar lawn daisy of Europe, Bellis perennis has a name that traces back to the Old English “day’s eye,” because its flowers open at dawn and close at dusk. Its small white petals and yellow centers have long been tied to innocence and simple joy in folklore and poetry.

Teddy Bear Sunflower

(Helianthus annuus ‘Teddy Bear’)

A compact, dwarf sunflower that looks nothing like its towering relatives. Instead of a flat face with a dark center, it carries fully double, fluffy golden-yellow blooms that resemble pom-poms, making it a favorite for containers, small gardens, and children’s plots.

Evening Sunflower

(Helianthus annuus ‘Evening Sun’)

Evening Sun is a tall, branching sunflower known for its warm sunset palette. Its blooms range through rust-red, bronze, mahogany, and gold, often with bicolor petals, and a single plant can produce many flowers over a long summer season.

ProCut® Red Sunflower

(Helianthus annuus ‘ProCut® Red’)

Part of the ProCut series bred for cut flowers, this sunflower has deep red petals around a dark center. The plants are pollen-free, so they stay tidy in a vase, and they flower quickly and uniformly, which is why florists and flower growers like them.

ProCut® White Nite Sunflower

(Helianthus annuus ‘ProCut® White Nite’)

White Nite brings an unusual look to the sunflower family, with pale creamy-white petals around a dark center. Another member of the pollen-free ProCut series, it is popular in bouquets for its soft, elegant color and clean, tidy flowers.

Firecracker Sunflower

(Helianthus annuus ‘Firecracker’)

Firecracker is a semi-dwarf, well-branched sunflower that bursts with bicolor blooms, with red-orange petals fading to golden yellow around chocolate-brown centers. It is pollen-free, grows well in containers or sunny borders, and may need a little support as it matures.

Soraya Sunflower

(Helianthus annuus ‘Soraya’)

Soraya is a classic, large-flowered sunflower with warm golden-orange petals surrounding a dark brown center. It grows tall and sturdy, is widely used as a cut flower, and has earned recognition among garden sunflowers for its reliable, cheerful blooms.

Vincent’s Choice Sunflower

(Helianthus annuus ‘Vincent’s Choice’)

Named in honor of Vincent van Gogh, whose paintings made sunflowers world famous, this variety has the classic look: bright golden-yellow petals around a dark center. It is a popular cut flower and a fitting tribute to the painter.

First Light Sunflower

(Helianthus salicifolius ‘First Light’)

Unlike most sunflowers on this page, First Light is a perennial. It comes from the willow-leaved sunflower, a native of the central United States, and stays at a manageable three to four feet while producing masses of golden-yellow daisy-like flowers from late summer into fall.
